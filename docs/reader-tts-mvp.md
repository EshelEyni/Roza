# Audiobook Mode — Local (Kokoro), OpenAI and System voices

Audiobook Mode uses the layout and behavior of RozaReader (the standalone Kokoro reader), built with Roza's reader styling: a top bar with Contents, Save offline and text size; the chapter text with sentence and word highlights; and a player with the narrator, the transport (chapter and sentence skip), volume, speed and export. The narrator picker keeps the `roza_audiobook_voice_modal_fixed.html` design.

The narrator picker offers three engines:

- **Local voices (Kokoro):** 28 English voices that run on the reader's own computer in the **Roza desktop app**. They are free and private, have exact word timing and work offline. They are installed on first use. On the website they show as "Desktop app only".
- **OpenAI voices:** AI-generated voices, made by the backend (see below).
- **System voices:** the browser's or device's own voices (`window.speechSynthesis`).

There is no Generate step: press Play and narration advances automatically through the book.

## Behavior

Open Reader menu → Audiobook Mode. The menu order remains Go to page, Review later, Sentence Mode, RSVP, Audiobook Mode, Settings.

- **Play/Pause:** starts at the current sentence/word from the existing reader CFI anchor. Reads continuously across sentences and linear EPUB sections. Long system-voice sentences are spoken in bounded chunks.
- **Previous/Next sentence** and **Previous/Next chapter:** move within the current section or across sections; chapters come from the book's table of contents. Playing continues; paused navigation stays paused. Clicking a sentence in the text reads from there.
- **Contents:** the top bar lists the book's chapters and jumps to one.
- **Seek:** the player bar moves to a word within the displayed EPUB section. Dragging commits on release; keyboard changes also work.
- **Speed:** opens the reference-style popover, 0.5×–2.5×. System speech restarts at the last reported word. OpenAI and local voices change the playback rate without regenerating audio (local voices keep their pitch). Speed is remembered on this device.
- **Volume:** a mute button and a slider. It applies at once to OpenAI and local voices; system speech picks it up from the next phrase. Volume is remembered on this device.
- **Text size:** A− and A+ in the top bar (16–27px), remembered on this device.
- **Voice:** the player's narrator button opens the **Choose a narrator** modal (centered desktop dialog / mobile bottom sheet). System voices update on asynchronous `voiceschanged` events, with search and an English-only filter. Local voices have gender and accent filters. Select a card, optionally preview it, then **Use this voice** to apply; Cancel/close/backdrop/Escape discard the staged choice. Browser-default narration remains available even if no voices are enumerated.
- **Back to the book:** the top bar's "Book" button closes the full view while narration continues in a **mini player** over the normal reader, and the page follows the spoken sentence. The mini player has previous/next sentence, play/pause, reopen and stop. Leaving the book or pressing stop ends the session.
- **Exit:** stops speech, restores focus/normal reader controls and returns the EPUB reader to the current sentence CFI.
- **Keyboard:** Space plays and pauses; ← → move by sentence; ⇧← ⇧→ move by chapter; + and − change the text size. Escape dismisses a popover first, then exits the mode. Focus stays within the open mode.

Text extraction reuses the existing sentence and CFI helpers, preserves paragraph grouping and inline text, and excludes scripts, hidden content and note-reference text. One spine section is shown at a time; titles come from existing Contents navigation rather than invented chapter numbers. Non-linear/empty sections are skipped during continuous reading.

The narrator modal pauses book narration before any preview. Closing it resumes from the same reading position only if the book was playing before opening; applying while already paused keeps it paused. Every engine previews the displayed fixed sample: “The morning arrived quietly, and the world seemed full of possibility.” OpenAI previews generate genuine audio through an authenticated, rate-limited backend endpoint; the browser cannot supply arbitrary preview text. Previews may incur API charges, are cached temporarily like narration, and show preparation/error states. Local previews are made once on the computer and kept. Previews stop on close, replacement, actual page navigation or errors; stale events cannot cancel resumed narration. Merely hiding the document does not stop narration or previews. Native dialog semantics, keyboard focus wrapping/return, viewport-height limits and 16px mobile search input support touch and keyboard use.

The selected engine (`system`, `openai` or `kokoro`), voice id/name and language are saved in the owned book's optional `audiobookNarrator` field, independently of reading progress. No migration is needed for existing books. Applying a narrator waits for the save; failures keep settings open with a retry message. A default system voice is also saved when first used. Refresh restores the book's choice. System voice enumeration gets a short grace period for asynchronous loading. If the saved voice is missing, OpenAI is disabled, a system voice reports unavailable, or a local voice is used on the website, on a computer without local voices, or for a non-English book, playback is blocked and the narrator settings open with a warning. Choose a valid replacement explicitly or exit Audiobook Mode; there is no silent substitution. The preference follows the book across devices, but device-specific voices may require choosing a replacement there.

## Virtualized text

Long chapters made the full-section view lag, so the chapter text is virtualized as in RozaReader (`@tanstack/react-virtual`): only about 100 paragraphs around the reading position are in the page, with estimated and then measured heights, while the scroll height covers the whole chapter and native scrolling reaches its end. Every rendered paragraph is exact book text. The reading position stays in view while narration plays; scrolling by hand pauses that for four seconds. Find-in-page only covers the paragraphs currently in the page.

Paragraphs are memoized, so opening the voice modal or changing player status does not re-render the chapter. Only the active sentence receives individual word elements, capped at a stable 64-word window around the current spoken position; text before and after that window remains exact plain text. Highlighting and seeking retain the original sentence/word offsets and CFI source.

## Local voices (Kokoro, desktop app)

Kokoro runs only in the Roza desktop app (`desktop/kokoro/`), never on the server. The page reaches it through `window.rozaDesktop.kokoro`, a small API exposed by the sandboxed `desktop/preload.cjs`. The main process only answers the app's own top-level page, never the book's iframes, and validates every argument.

**Install on first use.** Choosing Local voices on a computer without them offers **Install local voices**. The installer works entirely inside `%APPDATA%\Roza\kokoro\runtime` (it follows the app data folder, so `--data-dir` moves it too): a pinned, checksum-verified `uv`; Python 3.11; pinned `kokoro`, `misaki[en]`, `soundfile`, `imageio-ffmpeg` and the spaCy English model; then the voice model and all 28 voices. espeak-ng comes from `espeakng-loader` and ffmpeg from `imageio-ffmpeg`, so nothing is added to `PATH`. It ends with a self-test that writes speech to a path with Hebrew letters. It needs about 3 GB free while installing and shows its stage and progress; Cancel stops it. **Remove local voices** deletes the runtime and all saved audio. Narration runs with `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`, so it never waits for the network.

**Narration.** The page sends each sentence's exact words (from Roza's own sentence split) and its pacing. Following RozaReader, pacing adds silence after each sentence by context (question, trailing line, paragraph end, speaker change, scene break, chapter end) and slows short narration lines slightly. One `service.py` process makes one sentence at a time, with listener requests ahead of read-ahead and offline saves. It runs 4 threads, so the computer stays responsive. Clips are cached under `%APPDATA%\Roza\kokoro\audio\<book>\<voice>\` with a hash of their text, words, pacing and format version, so changed text never plays stale audio. They are served on the app's own origin at `/__kokoro/…` with byte ranges (seeking works, no CSP change). Three sentences ahead are made and preloaded; near a section's end the next section's first sentence is made too. Word highlights follow the clip's real word timings.

**Save offline and export (local voices only).** Save offline saves a chapter or the whole book, in order and in the background; each chapter is ready as soon as it finishes, and stopping keeps finished audio. Export makes one M4A, MP4 or MP3 file of a chapter or the book, with chapter markers, in a folder picked in the system dialog (default `Music\Roza Audiobooks`). Both first read the whole book once per session as separate documents, so the page view is not disturbed. With an OpenAI or system narrator, these dialogs explain that they need a local voice.

**English only.** Kokoro's voices are English. Local voices are unavailable when the EPUB's language tag is not English, or when an untagged book's text is mostly non-Latin (for example Hebrew).

**Backend and release order.** The backend accepts `kokoro` as a narrator engine (validated against its own voice list). Deploy the backend before releasing a desktop build with local voices; an older backend rejects the narrator, and the picker shows a save error. For development, `npm run desktop:dev` (repo root, `scripts/desktop-dev.cjs`) does everything in one command: it installs missing packages and the Electron binary, uses the local backend on port 3030 (starting it if needed and stopping it when the app closes), rebuilds `desktop/renderer` only when the frontend changed (`-- --rebuild` forces it), and opens the app from source with no installer build. The app passes the switches `--api-origin=http://localhost:3030 --data-dir=Roza-Dev`, so the installed app's sign-in and local voices are untouched; packaged builds ignore both.

## Real synchronization and timing limits

System sentence highlighting and advancement use real speech events. Dark system-word highlights use native word-boundary events only; if a voice does not emit boundaries, sentence follow-along still works. OpenAI word highlights use validated ASR timestamps and the actual audio clock, as described below. Local voices use the word timings Kokoro reports for each clip. No engine guesses words from a reading-speed timer. The footer indicates the available follow-along mode.

Native mobile pause/resume behavior varies, so Pause cancels the current utterance and retains the last real word boundary. Play resumes there. Without boundary events, it repeats the current short speech chunk. Voice/speed/seek changes also cancel the old utterance; stale callbacks cannot advance playback.

Web Speech has no exact recording duration or audio seek timeline. Elapsed/remaining values are explicitly **estimated section timing**, based on text progress and 180 words/minute adjusted by speed. They are not measured audio timestamps or whole-book duration. The progress bar seeks through text within the current section; Previous/Next and automatic playback cross sections.

Document visibility changes do not intentionally pause speech or cancel look-ahead. Actual page navigation/unmount still cleans up playback and outstanding requests. The OS/browser can independently suspend audio, JavaScript or networking, particularly on iPhone Safari/PWA; background/locked-screen playback is not guaranteed. Audio-context interruptions are still reported and can require pressing Play. There are no offline downloads or separate persistent audiobook progress. Normal EPUB position saving is reused when exiting.

## OpenAI playback

The existing Node backend calls OpenAI; EC2 performs no model inference and needs no Python service or second container. See [backend setup and API contract](../backend/docs/audiobook-openai-api.md). Install both frontend/backend dependencies. Enable `READER_AUDIOBOOK_ENABLED=true` in the backend's normal environment and configure `OPENAI_API_KEY` (or `READER_AUDIOBOOK_OPENAI_API_KEY`). Restart only your local development backend after configuring it. No key belongs in a frontend/Vite environment variable. The capability endpoint checks configuration, not OpenAI account permissions or credit.

Open Reader menu → Audiobook Mode → Choose narrator → OpenAI voices → choose a voice → Use this voice → Play. The picker discloses AI-generated speech and API charges. Choosing a voice does not make a paid preview request; its separate play icon explicitly generates a sample. With the feature disabled or configuration unavailable, System voices remain available for explicit selection, and the picker explains why OpenAI is unavailable.

The frontend sends precise source CFIs, never arbitrary text or a provider key. The server extracts the passage from the owned EPUB and returns authenticated WAV audio. Long sentences are split automatically at at most 600 UTF-16 characters, preserving inline-node/whitespace offsets and Unicode boundaries. A resumed/seeked passage begins at the selected word. The browser verifies returned source text before playback; unusual EPUB source differences (for example, embedded note references excluded by the reader) stop with an explanation and a System voices option.

Web Audio is unlocked in the Play/apply user gesture before fetching. Adjacent short sentences and paragraphs are grouped into passages of at most 600 characters. While the current audio plays, the next passage is generated and decoded, including the first passage of the next linear section. Normal advancement consumes that prepared audio (or waits for the same in-flight request), rather than starting generation after every sentence. Only one provider request runs at a time; no full chapters/books are generated. Initial loading is still required, and a buffer underrun can occur if generation/network latency exceeds the current audio's remaining duration. This is bounded look-ahead, not a gapless playback guarantee.

Realtime generation itself supplies no word timestamps. For book narration, the backend now makes one additional `whisper-1` transcription request for word timestamps after generating audio. It conservatively matches recognized words to exact UTF-16 source offsets, including repeated words, punctuation, inline sentences, contractions and Unicode. Missing/changed words, ambiguous merged words, invalid timestamps, errors or a 10-second timeout fall back to passage highlighting without failing narration or guessing timing. Previews skip alignment. Audio and timing metadata share the bounded temporary cache and cancellation scope; there are no automatic alignment retries.

Word highlights follow `AudioContext.currentTime` against those timestamp intervals, adjusted for playback rate and actual pause/resume offsets. Silence clears the active word. Frames update only when the timed word changes, and hidden/throttled pages catch up to current audio time when rendering resumes. Advancing into another sentence changes the visible word/sentence but retains the original audio-buffer origin, so pause/resume does not regenerate the passage. Seeking/voice changes/exit cancel old highlight frames. Text and native scrolling remain complete, and individual word elements remain capped at 64. Section time values remain text-based estimates, while text progress advances at validated word boundaries when available.

**Cost and retirement:** `READER_AUDIOBOOK_WORD_TIMINGS` defaults to `true`; set it to `false` in the backend environment to skip the extra transcription cost/latency. At current Whisper pricing the additional cost is approximately $0.006 per audio minute ($0.36/hour), excluding original narration. No extra key/container/model download is needed. OpenAI schedules `whisper-1` retirement for **February 26, 2027**; this isolated adapter is temporary and must be replaced with a timestamp-capable service before then. If access ends first, narration continues with passage highlighting. ASR word boundaries are estimates measured from the audio, not guaranteed phoneme-perfect alignment. See [timestamp support](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/speech-to-text) and [pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing).

Pause retains the current decoded buffer and its actual playback offset; resume and speed changes do not require regeneration of that passage. Pause, seeking, changing narrator, or exiting aborts outstanding look-ahead requests and cancels the corresponding server job. Opening the narrator modal pauses playback; Cancel resumes the same buffer if narration had been playing. At most two decoded passages (current and next) are retained in memory. Look-ahead may incur a charge for one passage the user stops before hearing. The backend also has a bounded, temporary, per-owner audio cache; there is no persistent browser audio cache. Prefetch failures let current audio finish and surface when playback reaches the failed passage, without an automatic paid retry. The user can retry Play or explicitly choose System voices.

Manual listening should compare the same passage using Previous sentence and each engine at 1×. Check initial playback, automatic advancement, seek into a long sentence, pause/resume, voice changes, speed, network failures, hide/return and exit. OpenAI requires internet and server-side account/model access. No model download is required on the phone.

## Local verification

1. `npm ci --prefix frontend`, then start Roza using its usual local backend/frontend setup.
2. Open an EPUB at a known position and enter Audiobook Mode from the reader menu.
3. Select an installed voice and press Play. Listen for actual speech. Check sentence/word follow-along, automatic scrolling, Pause/Play, Previous/Next, seeking, speed, and crossing a section.
4. Test a voice without word-boundary support: sentence highlights should continue without fabricated word motion.
5. Exit and confirm the normal reader returns to the current sentence. Check app hide/return, voice errors, rotation, safe areas, and narrow/landscape viewports.
